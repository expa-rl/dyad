Dyad: Extending Large Language Models with Native Typed Decision-Making

TL;DR: Dyad extends pretrained LLMs with native typed decision-making over dynamic action spaces, alongside language reasoning. It supports both frozen-LLM adaptation and joint optimization to build more capable agents.

1. Modular adaptation

Improve existing LLMs without updating them

Freeze the pretrained LLM and train only the action encoder. Dyad improves performance in most evaluated settings, with both Qwen models improving on all four unseen environments.

2. Joint optimization

Stronger agents through joint optimization

Training the LLM and action encoder together improves both GRPO and GiGPO in-domain. Across unseen environments, Dyad outperforms the corresponding RL baseline in 41 of 48 comparisons.

3. General capabilities

Better retention of general capabilities

The agent gains are accompanied by generally stronger knowledge, mathematical reasoning and coding performance than the corresponding RL baselines. After CodeGym training, both Dyad variants exceed the original models on every general-capability evaluation.

Approach

How Dyad works

The LLM represents the evolving interaction state, while an environment-conditioned action encoder represents the available actions independently. A dedicated interaction token triggers typed action selection.

State and action representations are computed independently. Action representations can be reused while their descriptions and environment context remain unchanged. Paper, Figure 2.

Frozen-LLM adaptation
Train only the action encoder; keep the pretrained LLM and its language head fixed.
Joint optimization
Train the LLM and action encoder together through environment interaction.
Further analysis

Learning Dyad. Supervised alignment initializes the action projector before agentic RL with GRPO or GiGPO. Section 3.

Training dynamics. Higher final validation rewards accompany shorter responses and more environment interactions. Figure 4.

System 1 evaluation and ablations. Dedicated decision benchmarks and component studies provide additional evidence. Sections 5.4–5.5.
